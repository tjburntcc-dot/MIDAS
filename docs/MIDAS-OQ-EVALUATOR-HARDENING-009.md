# MIDAS-OQ-EVALUATOR-HARDENING-009 amendment

## Discovery and preservation

The first Astra canary exposed an architecture issue in protocol-007, not a change in substantive scoring policy. Protocol-007 declared that low confidence, contradiction, insufficient evidence, ambiguity, critical judgment, deterministic conflict, margin risk, close aggregate risk, unsupported reasoning, and replay instability could lead to a second evaluator; its runtime validator enforced some local forward implications but did not mechanically establish every declared escalation rule, reverse implication, or campaign-level premise. Protocol-007, packet-01, the raw canary, and validation evidence remain immutable historical evidence. This contributes to final MIDAS by preserving the audit trail that revealed the repair instead of obscuring it.

## Root cause and category classification

`LOW_CONFIDENCE`, `MATERIAL_CONTRADICTION`, `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE`, `JUDGMENTAL_CRITICAL_FAILURE`, `AMBIGUITY`, and `UNSUPPORTED_REASONING` are evaluator-observable review signals. `DETERMINISTIC_CONFLICT` is a mechanically validated response condition after MIDAS applies case gates. `MATERIAL_MARGIN_RISK`, `CLOSE_AGGREGATE_RISK`, and `REPLAY_INSTABILITY` are campaign-level consequences or invalid/redundant evaluator categories: they cannot be authoritatively determined from one response before all primary coverage exists. This contributes to final MIDAS by making each escalation fact come from the actor with the information needed to establish it.

In particular, one response cannot know another run's quality, whether a 75-point threshold is crossed, whether the two-run spread exceeds eight, or whether an arm difference meets five points. `second_evaluator_required` mixed honest local observation with campaign governance. Ordinary ambiguity or insufficient evidence may simply lower the anchored score while every valid alternative remains on the same side of every frozen boundary. A blanket rule would multiply premium-model calls for signals that cannot change the eventual decision. This contributes to final MIDAS by avoiding paid work that adds no decision value.

## Governing amendment

Protocol-009 separates local signals from MIDAS governance and uses bounded value-of-information analysis at frozen boundaries: competence 75, maximum run-quality spread 8, practical quality margin 5, two runs per arm, 28 cases per run, and no critical failures. It selects the minimal sufficient response/dimension set, deduplicates cases, keeps primary judgments hidden from secondary reviewers, and leaves uncertainty unresolved when review cannot settle it. This contributes to final MIDAS by making independent review an economically intelligent control rather than a reflex.

The amendment changes escalation mechanics only. It does not alter dimensions, weights, anchors, gates, permitted evidence, blindness, quality-only scope, or the telemetry exclusion; it makes no economic conclusion. Existing protocol-007 scorecards are not silently migratable, so a fresh successor canary is required. Successor packets are regenerated separately and ignored; they never overwrite protocol-007 material. This contributes to final MIDAS by keeping the frozen measurement standard and evidence boundary intact while strengthening control.
