# First owned venture: estimate-path check

Prepared September 11, 2026 by the development assistant using public read-only research. This is a reversible venture hypothesis, not a MIDAS runtime research result. No outreach, purchases, registrations or customer tests were executed.

## Recommendation and critique

Provisionally offer a fixed-scope website estimate-path check to owner-operated residential cleaning firms with an existing website and estimate-request route. The deliverable is a prioritized, reproducible issue report and a handoff ticket for the owner's existing webmaster. Start as a manual service, not a new SaaS. The assumed pilot price is USD 149, not a verified market price or an authorized offer.

The bottleneck is buyer access and willingness to pay, not report generation. Free tools, an existing webmaster or a quick owner check may be sufficient substitutes. An external report is low value if nobody can fix it. Do not promise lost-lead recovery or use hypothetical conversion uplifts. The scope excludes form submission, profile access, edits, calls and credentials. Any later authorized end-to-end delivery test needs a separate exact scope.

One existing website; homepage to estimate/contact route; desktop plus narrow mobile; up to five pages; up to five prioritized issues; screenshots and reproducible steps; one correction round on factual inaccuracies. Deliver as a short report plus webmaster tickets. No implementation, uptime monitoring, accessibility/legal certification, security testing, SEO or conversion guarantees. Schedule only after estimating founder effort and agreeing access with the owner. Sample: SAMPLE-REPORT.md.

## Buyer interview: 15-minute guide

Ask about the last real event before showing the sample. Do not read leading questions or present invented defect counts.

1. How did your last three estimate requests reach you? What can you verify, and what is unknown?
2. When was the last time a customer said the contact process failed or was confusing? What happened next? If never, record that.
3. Who checks the website after changes, and who fixes an issue? What does that process cost in time or money?
4. What would make an outside report unnecessary? What other work is more urgent?
5. Show the labeled sample. Which part would you use, ignore or need changed? Ask them to point to a specific next action.
6. Would a fixed-scope USD 149 pilot be worth evaluating? What decision process and evidence would be required? This is a price test, not a sale or commitment.
7. May we follow up with the final scope? Separately record permission; do not infer consent from participation.

## Bounded validation and decision rules

After founder confirms access and availability, propose up to five qualified conversations over a maximum of two founder hours of acquisition work plus 75 minutes of interviews. This is a proposed workload, not a promise of availability. Stop recruiting at either bound; do not keep extending until feedback looks positive.

**Advance to separately approved pilot:** at least two of five can describe a concrete recent workflow problem or costly checking process, at least one can name a person who would use/fix the report and explicitly requests a written USD 149 pilot scope. This is directional buying interest only. Commercial validation requires actual accepted paid work later; no payment is requested now.

**Revise:** buyer confirms problem but cannot act on a report, or prefers an agency handoff. Change scope/channel once, document evidence and price uncertainty; do not call the original hypothesis validated.

**Stop this offer:** no qualified buyer access within the bounded effort, no actionable need across five conversations, or measured production/review burden makes the proposed price unattractive. Five conversations are directional discovery, not a population estimate. Preserve negative and nonresponses separately.
