## Smallest useful comparison

Use six open synthetic business episodes, each run once under each configuration: **12 workflow runs**, paired by identical episode facts and service fault schedule. No reliability estimate, population confidence interval or claim of optimal team size from this small pilot. Counterbalance order by episode and reset all trial/service state. Finish two representative episodes under both configurations before releasing the other four; stop on authority/accounting defects or unusable review packets.

Proposed coverage: two viable opportunities with different cost evidence, one unprofitable opportunity, one missing-cost case, one conflicting-publication-policy case, and one viable opportunity with an effect-before-response timeout requiring reconciliation. Episode parameters and expected decisions must be frozen before either configuration runs. Include borderline arithmetic only where a documented business threshold makes it meaningful, not to manufacture failures. One publication path must validate the full artifact against declared customer-facing requirements, not keywords alone.

**Strong single-agent baseline:** one Astra High general-purpose workflow owner handles investigate, decide, operate and verify, using an excellent master procedure covering evidence acquisition, provenance, money arithmetic, current-policy precedence, uncertainty, approval scope, least action, exact artifact readback and obligations. It gets all useful general checklists and phase-specific contracts. Verification is a fresh call with the same observable artifact/receipt package available to the team verifier. No hidden reviewer conclusions are fed back into one condition only.

**Smallest plausible MIDAS team:** two model roles plus the same human approval authority used by the baseline:

| Member | Necessary job | Model calls per completed positive episode |
|---|---|---:|
| Workflow owner | Request missing evidence, compare allowed options, produce decision and artifact | 3: investigate, decide, operate |

The workflow owner maps to existing analyst/operator task slots; the verifier maps to the existing verifier slot. This is a fixed small team hypothesis, not a team optimizer. The second role is justified only by a potentially valuable assurance boundary; it is not automatically better because it is named a specialist. Both baseline and team receive the same verification evidence. The treatment is the recorded role/procedure allocation, not extra information, hidden tools or extra paid calls. Equalize the general guidance; do not reserve correctness rules for the specialist prompt.

## Truth, ambiguity and independent verification

Truth comes from frozen synthetic source records, declared benefit/cost assumptions and publication-policy versions; expected contribution is computed with integer minor units. “Positive contribution” means a modeled decision under those assumptions, not realized value. The correct action is the best eligible positive contribution, no-go when no eligible option is positive, and qualified blocking when required evidence is missing/conflicting. Define tie handling and policy priority before execution.

Every expected behavior must be traceable to the worker-visible brief, policy or tool contract. Before launch, review that traceability with the workflow owner and test examples/counterexamples for refund/approval terminology, publication permission, evidence freshness, missing data, economics, and what counts as completed delivery. The full artifact requirements—including billing-status and payment-timing coverage—must be in the task contract. Internal verification-ticket keyword rules are not sufficient semantic ground truth and must not be hidden requirements.

The service's authenticated receipt/readback, artifact hash, effect count and ledger obligations supply verification independent of model self-report. A fresh model verifier is not a confidential holdout boundary or independent human validation. A competent human reviews semantic usefulness and policy fidelity with condition labels hidden where practical. Same-host development sources are open material; no protected final labels are created here. Preserve all failed/unknown observations and assistance disclosures. If no genuinely independent human reviewer is available, keep the semantic result explicitly assisted and defer independent qualification.

## Measurements and decisions

Primary outcome: an accepted end-to-end operational result, consisting of an evidence-supported decision, correct policy/authority behavior, usable artifact where action is warranted, exact approved delivery/readback and no unresolved obligations; justified rejection/blocking counts as correct when expected by the case. No-go episodes must have zero writes. Quality outranks cost.

Record every admitted attempt/failure, wrong action, unsupported economic assertion, missed/extra referral, preapproval write, duplicate effect, artifact mismatch, false completion and unresolved-effect state. Independently score source support, decision usefulness and artifact quality. Record model/phase latency, total active machine time, wall time including approval waits, actual review/correction durations, all role tokens, provisional reservations, reconciled provider charges and cost per accepted workflow outcome. Report machine latency separately from human wait time. Missing measurements remain missing.

Compare paired **episode** outcomes, not 48 calls as independent cases. Report the six differences and episode/family limits rather than a broad significance claim. Pre-register: advance the fixed team to a larger test only if it has more accepted episodes than the baseline, no extra critical failures, and meets the agreed latency/correction/resource constraints. An unresolved shared specification defect invalidates the affected causal comparison. With missing independent effort/billing, resource qualification remains incomplete even if a quality signal appears.

| Observed result | What changes |
|---|---|
| Both configurations succeed similarly | Prefer the simpler single-agent allocation unless the verifier supplies another measured benefit; no specialization victory |
| Team catches consequential errors at acceptable total cost | Retain that verifier procedure as an experimental candidate and test it on new independent episodes; do not certify it |
| Both fail at the same stage | Repair context, tool contract or shared policy before prompt optimization |
| Baseline outperforms team | Remove unnecessary handoffs/roles; retain baseline and record coordination overhead |
| Inconclusive or mixed | Name the specific disputed phase/metric and propose only the smallest discriminating follow-up; no automatic expansion |

The capability output is a versioned record of evidence acquisition, policy/economic decision, artifact delivery and verification competence, with context/tool requirements, authority boundaries, human dependencies, costs and known failure modes. That informs future team assignment. This experiment does not establish general business-building ability or globally optimal team assembly.
